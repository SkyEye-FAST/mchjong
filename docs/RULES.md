# Rules and presets

[Documentation](README.md) | [Playing guide](PLAYING.md)

Choose [Riichi](#riichi), [MCR](#mcr), [Sichuan mahjong](#sichuan-mahjong) or
[Taiwanese mahjong](#taiwanese-mahjong) in the lobby before readying. Each uses its own tile stock,
winning conditions and settlement. Room controls, permissions and Riichi convenience
hints are covered in the [Playing guide](PLAYING.md).

Examples use `m`, `p` and `s` for characters, circles and bamboo. `1z` through `7z`
mean East, South, West, North, White, Green and Red. `q` denotes flowers.
Some award examples show the relevant groups. Complete the rest of the hand
and meet the stated winning circumstances. Ordinary patterns permit exposed
groups unless their description requires a closed hand. A concealed kong keeps
the hand closed. Each listed value applies to the illustrated award. A complete
hand's total includes all applicable awards.

## Riichi

### From dealing to settlement

Four-player Riichi uses four copies of each suited tile and each of the seven honors, for 136 tiles. Everyone receives 13 tiles. The dealer receives one extra tile and discards first. Turns move counterclockwise.

The last 14 tiles form the dead wall for replacement draws and dora indicators. Flowers are not used.

Start with Winning Hands, then Riichi, Calls & Furiten. Normally, a winning hand needs one yaku han. Dora adds han after that requirement is met.

Discard after Chi or Pon. A kan uses four tiles as one group, so take a replacement. An added kan may be robbed. A concealed kan keeps your hand closed.

The dealer repeats after winning, or after an exhaustive draw when tenpai. Otherwise the deal moves on. Draws and repeats add a honba. A nondealer win resets it.

Noten players share a 3,000-point payment to tenpai players: one receives 3,000, two receive 1,500 each, or three receive 1,000 each. All tenpai or all noten means no payment.

Normally play East and South, with four dealer positions per wind. Repeats add hands. Mahjong Soul and Tenhou also offer East-only matches.

Online presets end after settlement if someone is below zero; zero remains playable. See Preset Differences for final-dealer stops and extension.

Examples use `m`, `p` and `s` for characters, circles and bamboo. Honors `1z`–`7z` represent East, South, West, North, White, Green and Red. Thus, `123m` is a sequence and `55z` is a White pair. Spaces separate groups. Each example illustrates the named condition.

Continue with Yaku, Yakuman, and Fu & Points.

Online presets allow abortive draws. On an uninterrupted first draw, holding nine distinct terminal or honor kinds lets you choose Nine Terminals. Four identical first wind discards before any call end the hand in an abortive draw.

Four riichi declarations or four kans by multiple players also end the hand in an abortive draw. The fourth-kan check follows the replacement draw and discard-win checks. Play continues when one player has all four kans. An abortive draw leaves noten payments unchanged, retains the dealer and adds one honba.

### Winning, calls, riichi and furiten

A standard winning shape is four groups plus one pair: 14 tiles. Groups are either sequences (three consecutive numbers of one suit) or triplets (three identical tiles). Honors can only form triplets.

Seven Pairs and Thirteen Orphans are exceptions. Winning on your own draw is tsumo; winning on another player's discard is ron.

To win, your hand needs at least one yaku on top of the shape (the minimum-han requirement). Start with closed riichi, All Simples, and the triplet yaku of your seat wind, round wind, and dragons. Dora adds han but never counts toward the minimum.

For practice, the host can enable Open Hands. Turn on Tenpai Hints in Room settings before starting to see your waits and remaining copies computed from the visible tiles in real time.

Declare Riichi when you are tenpai with a closed hand. Pick a tile, discard it and post a 1,000-point stick. The hand then locks, restricting subsequent actions to drawing, discarding, winning or declaring a concealed kan.

Chi builds a sequence from the previous player's discard. Pon and Kan build triplets or quads from any player's discard. A call opens the hand, so scoring uses open-hand yaku eligibility and reduces kuisagari yaku by one han.

If any tile you are waiting on is among your own discards, you are in discard furiten and cannot ron on any of your waits. Passing a winning discard gives you same-turn furiten. After riichi it lasts until the hand ends.

You can still win by tsumo while in furiten. The red furiten badge on screen always tells you your current state.

Online riichi needs 1,000 points and at least four live tiles (three in sanma). M.League and JPML A need one live tile. WRC allows zero. Competition presets do not require 1,000 points already in hand.

Temporary furiten clearance depends on two independent options. **Own Draw Clears
Temporary Furiten** clears it on the affected player's next draw when enabled, or
on that player's discard following a draw when disabled. **Own Calls Clear
Temporary Furiten** additionally clears it on that player's chi, pon or open kan.
Another player's call never clears it. Neither option clears discard furiten or
post-riichi furiten. A discard after chi or pon is not a draw-and-discard turn.

| Preset | Own draw | Own chi, pon or open kan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mahjong Soul, four or three players | Clears temporary furiten | Does not clear it |
| Tenhou, four or three players | Clears on the following discard | Does not clear it |
| M.League | Clears on the following discard | Does not clear it |
| JPML A | Clears on the following discard | Does not clear it |
| WRC | Clears temporary furiten | Clears temporary furiten |

The [Mahjong Soul FAQ](https://mahjongsoul.com/faq) specifies the next own draw.
The [Tenhou manual](https://tenhou.net/man/) specifies discard-time clearance and
excludes reverse-order calls. [M.League rules](https://m-league.jp/about/),
chapter 5, section 5, require completion of the player's draw-and-discard turn.
[WRC 2025 rules](https://www.worldriichi.org/wrc-rules), section 8.11.2, allow the
player's next draw or call. The [JPML published rules](https://www.ma-jan.or.jp/activity/game_rule.html)
prohibit same-turn win selection but do not specify the action that ends it.
The JPML A preset retains draw-and-discard clearance on that unresolved timing point.
Riichi kans must keep the winning tile types; see Preset Differences for further restrictions.

### Fu, points and dora

Regular hands start at 20 fu. Add ten fu for closed ron or two fu for tsumo. Pinfu tsumo remains at 20 fu, and Seven Pairs remains at 25 fu. Round other totals up to the next ten fu.

A simple-tile open pung, closed pung, open kan or closed kan adds 2, 4, 8 or 16 fu respectively. Double these values for terminals or honors. A pung completed by ron counts as open when calculating fu.

A value pair adds two fu, or four fu for a double wind under the online presets. An edge, closed or pair wait adds two fu. Two-sided and paired waits add zero fu. An open hand with no other fu scores thirty fu.

Below mangan, basic points = fu × 2^(han + 2). Ron pays four times the basic points to a nondealer or six times to the dealer. For a nondealer tsumo, the dealer pays twice the basic points and each other opponent pays the basic points. For a dealer tsumo, each opponent pays twice the basic points. Round each payment up to the next 100 points.

A nondealer's three-han, forty-fu hand has 1,280 basic points. Ron pays 5,200 points. Tsumo collects 2,600 points from the dealer and 1,300 points from each other opponent. Each honba adds 300 points to ron or 100 points per tsumo payer. The winner takes the riichi deposits.

A two-han Seven Pairs ron pays 1,600 points to a nondealer, using the fixed 25 fu.

Mangan applies at five han or 2,000 basic points. Mangan ron pays 8,000 points to a nondealer or 12,000 points to the dealer. Six–seven han pays 1.5 times mangan, eight–ten han pays twice mangan, and eleven–twelve han pays three times mangan.

Normally, thirteen or more han gives counted yakuman, worth four times mangan. M.League caps ordinary hands at three times mangan. Natural yakuman uses its own scoring path and can compound.

Dora follows the indicator. A nine indicates the one of the same suit. Winds cycle East–South–West–North, and dragons cycle White–Green–Red. Each copy of dora adds one han. Each red five adds one bonus han.

Riichi wins reveal ura. Kans add indicators and corresponding ura. JPML A omits ippatsu, ura, and kan dora. Bonuses never replace a qualifying yaku.

### Three-player play

Sanma uses 108 tiles by removing `2m`–`8m`, and its legal calls are Pon and Kan. The dealer starts with fourteen tiles, and each nondealer starts with thirteen tiles. East, South and West give three dealer positions per wind.

Keep North as an ordinary tile or extract it for a replacement and one bonus han. Extraction keeps the hand closed but supplies no yaku. 1m and 9m indicate each other as dora.

North extraction interrupts ippatsu and can be won by an eligible opponent.
In the Mahjong Soul and Tenhou three-player presets, winning on its replacement
counts as Rinshan Kaihou. Extractions share replacement capacity with kans.
The [Tenhou manual](https://tenhou.net/man/) describes the replacement after an
added kan or North extraction as a rinshan draw. North extraction does not declare
a kan, so its following discard does not qualify for Kanburi. Ron on the extracted
North does not award Chankan. The engine tracks replacement-draw eligibility and
kan provenance separately, including across saved games.

Tsumo charges the two present opponents their usual four-player shares. The absent player's share remains unpaid. For a nondealer mangan, the dealer pays 4,000 points and the other opponent pays 2,000 points. The noten payment pool is 2,000 points.

### Preset options and custom rules

Open **Match Rules** from the lobby's Match Settings or Settings → Room. Riichi
rules use six fixed categories: Basic Settings, Points and Ranking, Win Scoring,
Match Progress, Riichi and Calls, and Liability Payments. Every player can inspect
the same categories. The host edits a local draft before play and submits it with
**Apply Rules**. A server acknowledgement confirms the change and clears readiness.
Rejected applications retain the draft. **Cancel** abandons unsent edits, while
**Reload** in changed-room feedback replaces the draft with current server rules.

Basic Settings contains player count, **Choose Rule Preset** and the supported
basic options. Mahjong Soul and Tenhou permit open tanyao, no/three/four red fives,
a one/two/four-yaku-han minimum and East-only or East–South matches within the named
preset, including when world policy disables custom rules. Their defaults are one
han and East–South. Other rule changes and award selection require custom-rule
permission. Custom status is derived from the complete draft.

A different Riichi preset retains its supported basic options and restores fixed
details and awards to its defaults. Reselecting the current preset preserves all
edits; **Restore Preset Defaults** is a separate action. Changing player count uses
the same preset family when it supports that count, otherwise it opens the available
presets for the target count. Canceling the choice retains the original draft.
Count changes require gathering and sufficient free seats and stock. Help, award
selection, category changes, pagination and resizing retain unfinished input.

Point fields use raw points in increments of 100. A placement bonus of 15000 means
+15.0 in the final standings. Starting points, return points and extension target
are independent; oka is derived from the return-minus-start difference and player
count. Fixed placement bonuses and every floating-player table entry are editable.
The floating count is the number of players at or above the return threshold.
Custom scoring, draw, furiten, kan, liability and ending options use their explicit
values. Unchanged presets retain the defaults below.

Red-five settings select the exact composition taken from a physical box. A
choice with insufficient red fives is disabled and shows a shortage tooltip.
The server rechecks stock when applying a proposal. These settings do not
manufacture, recolor or remove stored tiles. See [replays](REPLAYS.md) for saved rule settings.

The minimum applies to every winning interpretation before selecting its payment.
Dora, ura dora, red fives and extracted-north bonuses do not satisfy it; natural
yakuman always qualify. Tenhou also excludes ippatsu from this minimum, as its
official manual specifies for bonus awards. The custom scoring section exposes
that distinction independently. A wait that cannot meet a higher minimum still
counts for furiten and formal tenpai; riichi does not guarantee an eligible ron.

East-only matches schedule one wind and East–South matches schedule two, using
three hands per wind in sanma and four in four-player play. The existing dealer
repeat and agari-yame rules apply at the selected last hand. When enabled,
extension adds at most one further wind (South or West). Dealer repeats retain
priority over another player's reaching the target. Starting points, return
points and placement bonuses do not change with match length.

**End on bankruptcy** is editable in **Match Progress** with custom-rule permission. Mahjong Soul and Tenhou enable it. M.League, JPML A and
WRC disable it. The check is strictly below zero, after hand settlement: zero
points remain playable and negative scores remain recorded. Changing this switch
away from its preset default marks the rules as custom.

### Preset specifications

Presets configure hanchan scoring and automated table play. Both JPML A and
WRC permit thirteen-or-more ordinary han to reach a fourfold mangan. M.League
caps ordinary hands at sanbaiman. A counted limit is distinct from a natural
yakuman and does not trigger natural-yakuman responsibility payments.

WRC renhou is compared with the ordinary hand and the higher payment wins. It
does not add five han to other yaku or dora. Riichi kans preserve waits. The
three competition presets also preserve the hand's interpretations. JPML A
additionally prohibits losing a yaku. WRC permits riichi with no live-wall tiles
remaining. M.League and JPML A require at least one.

Scoring evaluates hand interpretations, yaku, fu and point tables. Candidate
interpretations are compared and selected by highest actual payment rather than
han count alone.

### Physical red fives

The selected rules specify the playing set. The first compatible box must cover
every required ordinary and red tile. Surplus tiles stay in the box. The selected
subset has matching material, back color and face design. Different boxes never
pool their contents. Four-player play uses four copies of each of the 34 faces.

| Composition | Red 5m | Red 5p | Red 5s |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| No reds | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Three reds | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| Four reds | 1 | 2 | 1 |

Mahjong Soul and Tenhou accept all three compositions as table variants.
M.League uses three reds. JPML A and WRC use no reds. Changing those fixed settings
creates custom rules. Sanma requires only the 108 played tiles: 2m through 8m,
including the red 5m, need not be present. The three/four-red options therefore
select two/three playable red fives in sanma.

Printing 136 or 144 blank tiles or reprinting a complete set produces no red
fives. New tables start with the no-red Mahjong Soul variant. Spare red fives may
be added alongside all four ordinary fives, allowing one box to cover multiple
choices. An ordinary five cannot substitute for a required red five, or vice versa.
One vanilla red dye crafts four red dora dyes; one red dora dye plus an ordinary
five creates its red version. One black dye crafts four undo dyes; one undo dye
plus a red five restores its ordinary face while retaining its other components.
The first compatible complete box supplies the table. Changing the rule preset
rechecks both stored boxes before Ready or Practice is offered.


### Custom Riichi and Taiwanese mahjong awards

Riichi and Taiwanese mahjong tables allow individual switches for every default award and
every custom award in the server catalog. In **Table rules**, open **Yaku Selection**,
toggle the desired rows, return and apply the rule draft. Awards from multiple sets
can be combined. **Restore Defaults** enables all default awards and disables all
custom awards; **Clear All** disables both. Existing preset options still govern
availability, such as open Tanyao and Nagashi Mangan.
Only the lobby host can apply changes, and the world must permit custom rules.
Changes clear readiness. Choosing a preset restores its default award selection.
External Bot services require the default award selection. Built-in Bots and wait hints
use the table's scoring rules; Bots do not plan arbitrary custom patterns.

The **Mahjong Soul Ancient Yaku** switch in the four-player Mahjong Soul preset
enables or disables all 14 bundled ancient awards. Individual awards can also be
changed in Yaku Selection. **Partial** means some ancient awards are enabled;
clicking the switch then enables the whole bundle. The bundle is unavailable in three-player rules.
Its [JSON definition](../engine/src/main/resources/yaku_sets/mahjong_soul_ancient.json)
is a complete data-pack authoring example, with ID `mahjongsoul:ancient`:

| Awards | Closed / open |
| --- | --- |
| 燕返、杠振、十二落抬 | 1 / 1 han |
| 五门齐、三连刻 | 2 / 2 han |
| 一色三同顺 | 3 / 2 han |
| 一筒摸月、九筒捞鱼 | 5 / 5 han |
| 人和、大数邻、大车轮、大竹林、石上三年 | 1 yakuman / unavailable |
| 大七星 | 2 yakuman / unavailable |

These bundled rules do not double-count a contained award: 一色三同顺 excludes
Iipeikou, 一筒摸月 excludes Haitei, 九筒捞鱼 excludes Houtei, and 大七星 excludes
Tsuuiisou. Disabling the ancient award permits its default component to score again.

Put a definition at `data/<namespace>/mchjong/yaku_sets/<name>.json` in a server
data pack and run `/reload`. The selectable ID is `<namespace>:<name>`; nested
paths are allowed. For example, `data/house/mchjong/yaku_sets/five_gates.json`:

```json
{
  "variant": "riichi",
  "name": {"en_us": "Five Gates", "ja_jp": "五門斉", "zh_cn": "五门齐", "zh_tw": "五門齊"},
  "awards": [{
    "id": "house:five_gates",
    "name": {"en_us": "Five Gates", "ja_jp": "五門斉", "zh_cn": "五门齐", "zh_tw": "五門齊"},
    "description": {
      "en_us": "Include all three suits, a wind and a dragon.",
      "ja_jp": "萬子、筒子、索子、風牌、三元牌をすべて含む。",
      "zh_cn": "包含万、筒、索、风牌和三元牌。",
      "zh_tw": "包含萬、筒、索、風牌和三元牌。"
    },
    "when": {"type": "categories", "values": ["man", "pin", "sou", "wind", "dragon"]},
    "award": {"type": "han", "closed": 3, "open": 2},
    "excludes": []
  }]
}
```

Every name and description requires all four languages. IDs must be namespaced,
lowercase resource IDs; `mchjong:` is reserved. A set contains 1–16 distinct awards.
An award grants `han` (0–13) or natural `yakuman` units (0–2), with separate
`closed` and `open` values. Zero disables that award for the corresponding hand;
both values cannot be zero. Concealed kans retain closed status.

For Taiwanese mahjong, set `"variant": "taiwan"` and use `"award": {"type": "tai", "value": 3}`.
Tai awards range from 1 to 1,000 and use the same value for closed and open hands;
use a `closed` condition to restrict an award. A table selects awards for its own
variant. Taiwanese mahjong supports regular five-meld hands only. Custom tai is added before
the table's tai cap, then uses its ordinary base/per-tai payment settings.
When enabled, Eight Flowers and Seven Robbing One use automatic triggers and separate
payment components. Their individual switches disable those triggers. Custom awards apply only to an accompanying ordinary
replacement-tile win, never to the flower victory alone.

Conditions read one winning interpretation, including the winning tile and fixed
melds. They never read opponents' concealed tiles or the wall. Custom awards do
not add winning shapes, change fu, change furiten or create automatic victories.
The scorer evaluates all interpretations and selects the largest actual payment.
Ordinary custom han count toward the minimum. Dora still cannot satisfy it.
Natural yakuman replace ordinary awards and bonuses and follow the table's
compound-yakuman setting. Each definition explicitly sets its own yakuman units.

Combine conditions with `{"all": [...]}`, `{"any": [...]}` or `{"not": {...}}`.
Leaves use `type`, optional `values`, and explicit inclusive `min`/`max` for counts.

| Type | Values and meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `shape` | `regular`. Riichi also permits `seven_pairs`, `thirteen_orphans` |
| `closed` | Exactly one string: `true` or `false` |
| `flags` | All listed circumstances must hold: `self_draw`, `discard`, `robbing_kong`, `last_draw`, `last_discard`, `kong_replacement`, `heavenly_win`, `earthly_win`, `human_win`. Riichi adds `riichi`, `double_riichi`, `ippatsu`, `riichi_discard`, `after_kong_discard`. Taiwanese mahjong adds `ready`, `heavenly_ready`, `earthly_ready`, `flower_replacement` |
| `categories` | Each listed category is present: `man`, `pin`, `sou`, `wind`, `dragon` |
| `tiles` | Count owned copies of the listed ordinary kinds, such as `["1m", "9m"]`; quads count four, extracted Norths are excluded |
| `groups` | Count matching groups; exactly three values select type (`any`, `sequence`, `triplet`, `quad`), concealment (`all`, `concealed`, `exposed`) and first kind (`1m` etc. or `*`) |
| `pair` | Regular interpretation's pair matches any listed ordinary kind |
| `wait` | Match any listed placement: `pair`, `two_sided`, `closed`, `edge`, `triplet`, `special`. The last is for Seven Pairs/Thirteen Orphans |
| `reds` | Riichi only: count owned red fives; no `values` |
| `flowers` | Taiwanese mahjong only: count owned flowers; no `values` |
| `same_sequences` | Largest number of identical sequences in this interpretation; zero when none; no `values` |
| `three_suit_sequences` | A sequence of the same starting rank occurs in all three suits; no `values` or bounds |
| `winning_tile` | Winning ordinary kind matches any listed kind |
| `open_melds` | Count declared open melds. Concealed kans and a ron-completed triplet do not count; no `values` |
| `consecutive_triplets` | Three consecutive triplets or quads in one suit; no `values` or bounds |

A ron-completed triplet is exposed for group counting. Pair and group conditions
do not reinterpret a special hand as a regular one. Conditions allow four levels
(root plus three child levels), 64 nodes per award and 16 children per logical
node. Each document and its normalized snapshot must fit 32,768 characters;
the catalog, including the bundled example, permits 32 sets, 131,072 normalized
characters and 196,608 UTF-8 bytes in total. Award IDs must be unique across the catalog. Invalid
definitions fail the reload; unknown fields and duplicate JSON keys are errors.

`excludes` lists other custom IDs in the same set or ordinary native Riichi IDs,
such as `mchjong:riichi/iipeikou` or `mchjong:riichi/pinhu`. The native suffix is its
`yaku.mchjong.*` translation suffix. A custom natural yakuman may also exclude
native yakuman. Nagashi and native Renhou cannot be excluded by a custom definition.
Exclusions cannot contain cycles or unknown IDs, and ordinary
awards cannot suppress custom yakuman. They apply simultaneously: if A excludes B
and B excludes C, matching enabled A and B removes both B and C. Disabled custom
awards never contribute exclusions. Native switches apply before choosing between
ordinary awards and yakuman, so disabling a yakuman leaves qualifying ordinary
interpretations available. Structural winning shapes and fu remain unchanged.
Taiwanese mahjong native IDs use `mchjong:taiwan/` followed by the lowercase pattern enum name,
such as `mchjong:taiwan/concealed` or `mchjong:taiwan/pinfu`. Eight Flowers and
Seven Robbing One cannot be excluded by another award; use their individual switches.

Reload changes the selectable catalog. Existing tables, saves and native replays
retain the selected sets' complete definitions, localized text and individual switches.
After a definition changes, remove its selected awards and select the current
catalog entries before applying. Riichi matches with any changed award selection
use [native replay export](REPLAYS.md), since Tenhou's format cannot represent these
awards. MCR and Sichuan mahjong do not support custom award sets.

## MCR

### From dealing to settlement

MCR uses four seats, 136 ordinary tiles and eight flowers, for 144 tiles in total. Each nondealer starts with thirteen tiles. The dealer starts with fourteen tiles and discards first. Turns move counterclockwise.

Replace opening flowers dealer-first, finishing one player before the next. Reveal each flower and replace it from the wall tail; repeat if another flower appears.

Chow uses the previous player's discard. Pung and melded kong use any opponent's discard. Wins take priority over pung or kong, followed by chow. Simultaneous wins go to the nearest player after the supplier.

Replace after a concealed kong. An added kong waits for robbery responses. Flower replacement alone is not a kong-replacement win, and flowers never form groups.

The regular shape is four groups and a pair. See Special Hands for Seven Pairs, Thirteen Orphans, knitted forms, and Knitted Straight.

You need eight non-flower points to win. Each flower then adds one point. A seven-point hand with two flowers remains below the minimum. An eight-point hand with two flowers qualifies and pays as ten points.

MCR uses its own winning conditions. Declaring a complete shape below eight non-flower points incurs the wrong-win penalty. Pay each opponent ten points and continue the hand with winning disabled for your seat.

Keep drawing, discarding, and calling. The restriction clears next hand. A Win button alone does not mean you have enough points.

On self-draw, each opponent pays eight plus the total hand score. On a discard, the discarder pays that amount and the other two pay eight each.

For an eight-point hand with two flowers, self-draw collects eighteen points from each opponent, for fifty-four points in total. A discard win collects eighteen points from the discarder and eight points from each other opponent, for thirty-four points in total. Payments use fan points directly.

Start at zero and play sixteen hands across East, South, West, and North. The dealer moves after every hand, even a dealer win or draw. Exhaustion has no ready payment; earlier wrong-win penalties remain.

All 81 fans appear in High Fans, Middle Fans, and Basic Fans.

### Special shapes and combinations

Seven Pairs allows four copies as two pairs. Thirteen Orphans uses thirteen terminal/honor kinds and a duplicate. Neither accepts melds.

Honors and Knitted Tiles selects fourteen distinct kinds from three-suit 147/258/369 and honors. All seven honors makes Greater Honors. Both are closed; no All Types or Concealed Hand, but self-draw adds Fully Concealed Hand.

Knitted Straight uses 147, 258, 369 in different suits plus one group and pair. You cannot chow the nine-tile body. The extra group may be open.

Wait fan checks the residual shape. Do not reuse a tile allocated to the body. A separate copy of the same kind may complete the residual wait.

Add compatible fans, but do not rescore parts included in a larger fan or rearrange the same tiles for another score. A combined group may link once more with an unused group.

Mixed Triple Chow does not also give Mixed Double Chows; Pure Straight does not add its Short Straight or terminal-chow pair. Choose the best scoring interpretation, not their sum.

Index numbers are fan values. Ordinary patterns allow open groups unless stated closed. Examples may show only the relevant groups. Complete the hand and meet the stated circumstances.

Examples use `m`, `p` and `s` for characters, circles and bamboo. Honors `1z`–`7z` represent East, South, West, North, White, Green and Red, and `q` denotes flowers. Three Kongs and Four Kongs may add awards for their concealed portions, subject to the kong-award exclusions. Two Concealed Kongs adds eight points, and the mixed concealed/exposed two-kong combination adds six points.

## Sichuan mahjong

### From dealing to settlement

Sichuan mahjong uses four seats and 108 suited tiles. Each nondealer receives thirteen tiles, and the dealer receives fourteen tiles. Choose one suit to void after the deal. Clear every tile of that suit before winning.

Discard void-suit tiles first. Pung and kong are available for the other suits.

MIL also asks you to secretly bind your first discard. Play that tile at your first discard turn. If you have none of the void suit, no tile is bound. All void suits are revealed together, but chosen tiles stay private.

T/TFMJ selects only the suit. Neither preset exchanges three tiles.

Win with four groups and a pair, or Seven Pairs. No minimum fan is required. Four identical tiles may count as two pairs.

In blood battle, winners retire while everyone else keeps playing. Three winners or wall exhaustion ends the hand. Several players may win one discard. Its supplier pays each separately.

Passing a win blocks another win of the same or lower fan, including after declining self-draw. Drawing again or becoming not ready clears it.

Compare fan after the cap. Two hands both capped at three fan remain equal even if one has more raw fan.

Hands worth zero, one, two or three fan pay a base value of 1, 2, 4 or 8 points respectively. The default cap is three fan. A discarder pays that base value. On self-draw, each active opponent pays the base value plus one point.

A two-fan Full Flush collects four points on a discard win. With all four players active, self-draw collects five points from each opponent, for fifteen points in total. After one opponent retires, self-draw collects ten points in total.

The first winner deals next. If the first win has several winners on one discard, its supplier deals. With no winner, the dealer stays. Both presets normally play eight hands and rank cumulative scores.

Continue with Fan Patterns and Kongs & Draws.

Examples use `m`, `p` and `s` for characters, circles and bamboo. Honors `1z`–`7z` represent East, South, West, North, White, Green and Red, and `q` denotes flowers. Thus, `123m` is a sequence and `55p` is a pair.

An example may show the relevant groups. Complete the variant's winning shape and meet the stated circumstances. Each listed value applies to that award, and the whole hand's score includes all applicable awards. Ordinary patterns allow exposed groups unless their description requires a closed hand.

### Kong income, draw checks and presets

A concealed kong collects two points from each active opponent. A discard kong collects two points from its supplier. Immediately adding the drawn fourth tile to a pung collects one point from each active opponent. A delayed added kong earns zero points.

Kong income is separate from winning fan. Replace after a kong. A robbed added kong does not complete or collect.

After a kong-related shooting discard, MIL refunds only the last kong to its original payers. T/TFMJ transfers the whole paid consecutive-kong chain to the winners.

If that chain earned five points and two players win, each receives three points. The shooter supplies the additional point needed for equal shares. Winning payments are settled separately.

MIL calls declared quads Kong and undeclared fours Root; T/TFMJ calls both Root. Each adds one fan.

If an added kong after a replacement is won, MIL treats it as shooting: the kong completes without income. T/TFMJ treats it as robbery and keeps the pung. MIL has longer East/West walls; T/TFMJ has longer South/North walls.

At exhaustion, remaining not-ready players pay each eligible ready player their highest structural base value. Do not include replacement or last-tile circumstances. Not-ready players also refund retained kong income.

If two eligible ready players have structural values of two and eight points, a not-ready player pays them two and eight points respectively. Retired winners are excluded. Three winners finish the hand before draw checks and refunds.

Keeping void tiles at exhaustion is a passive flower pig: treat it as not ready and refund kongs. A referee-called active violation deducts 24 competition points, forbids wins, and refunds kong income; that penalty pays nobody.

A sanctioned ready player neither receives nor pays ready payments. Completed call transfers stay final. Custom rules can change caps, bonuses, kong amounts, and match length.

## Taiwanese mahjong

### From dealing to settlement

Taiwanese mahjong uses four seats. The Pocket preset adds eight flowers to the 136 ordinary tiles. The Southern preset uses the ordinary tiles alone. At least one human can play with built-in Bots.

Deal four packets of four tiles to each player, then one extra tile to the dealer. The dealer discards from a seventeen-tile hand. A regular winning hand needs five groups and a pair.

Opening flowers are replaced in dealer-first rounds; flowers drawn as replacements wait for the next round. During play, reveal and replace flowers from the tail until you draw an ordinary tile.

Chow uses the previous player's discard. Pung and open kong use any opponent's discard. Wins take priority over pung or kong, followed by chow. The nearest winning claimant after the supplier wins.

Discard after chow or pung. All kongs take a replacement. Only an added kong can be robbed. A kong itself has no immediate payment.

Declare ready by discarding a tile that leaves the hand waiting. The declaration locks subsequent discards to the drawn tile and ends call and kong eligibility. The declaration is optional and costs no deposit.

Passing a win blocks both discard and self-draw wins. Drawing alone does not clear it. Discard a kind outside your waits before that draw or call, or complete an added kong.

Normally, the reserve contains sixteen tiles plus one per completed kong. A fixed sixteen-tile reserve is also available. Ordinary and replacement draws use tiles outside the reserve. The hand ends in exhaustion when a required replacement is unavailable.

Complete sixteen dealer positions across East, South, West, and North. Dealer wins and draws add a repeat. A nondealer win moves the deal and clears repeats, including at the final North position.

Scores start at zero. There are no ready payments, abortive draws, bankruptcy, or dealer-win stops. See Flowers, Base & Tai and Tai Patterns.

Examples use `m`, `p` and `s` for characters, circles and bamboo. Honors `1z`–`7z` represent East, South, West, North, White, Green and Red, and `q` denotes flowers. Thus, `123m` is a sequence and `55p` is a pair.

An example may show the relevant groups. Complete the variant's winning shape and meet the stated circumstances. Each listed value applies to that award, and the whole hand's score includes all applicable awards. Ordinary patterns allow exposed groups unless their description requires a closed hand.

### Payments, flowers and presets

A discard win charges the discarder, while a self-draw charges all three opponents. Each payer owes the base payment plus the payment per tai multiplied by the payable tai. The base payment and payment per tai each default to one accounting unit.

The Pocket preset adds one dealer tai when the winner or payer is the dealer, plus two tai per dealer repeat. The Southern preset omits the dealer tai and retains two tai per repeat.

Under the Pocket preset, a nondealer self-draw worth three tai during one dealer repeat collects four accounting units from each nondealer and seven from the dealer. The dealer's payment is 1 + 3 + 1 + 2, and the winner collects fifteen units in total.

The Southern preset caps hand tai at four before dealer and repeat-dealer additions. A dealer winning a hand worth eight raw tai during one repeat collects 1 + 4 + 2 = 7 accounting units from each liable payer.

Eight Flowers and Seven Robs One each award eight tai without a regular 17-tile win. Eight Flowers charges all three. Seven Robs One charges the lone-flower holder.

Your seventh or eighth flower waits for replacement. An opponent drawing the last lone flower triggers Seven Robs One immediately. Opening flower wins wait until all replacement rounds finish.

Flower wins are automatic and bypass ordinary passing. A regular replacement win adds its own tai. Seven Robs One charges flower tai to one payer and regular self-draw tai to all three. Each owes only one base.

Pocket scores seat flowers, flower sets, and single waits; flower replacement can score Replacement Win. Southern has no flowers or Single Wait tai, kong-only Replacement Win, and a four-tai cap.

## Rule sources

Riichi presets select Mahjong Soul, [Tenhou](https://tenhou.net/man/), M.League,
JPML A and WRC rules with the table options described above. Preset names do not
change a custom rule after it has been edited.

MCR follows the World Mahjong Organization's 2014 *Mahjong Competition Rules*
([Chinese rulebook](https://www.mindmahjong.com/adobe/ZC2021.pdf)), including the
8-point Two Concealed Kongs and 6-point mixed concealed/exposed kong combination.
The latter is covered by Two Melded Kongs.

Sichuan mahjong offers [MIL SBR trial rules (2025)](https://mahjong-mil.org/) and
[T/TFMJ 01—2024](https://www.ttbz.org.cn/Home/PdfFileStreamGet/c3QsMTEyODcw).
Both table presets use eight hands; MIL leaves event length to the competition,
while T/TFMJ permits an eight-hand or timed format. Their selected differences
are described above.

Taiwanese mahjong presets combine the selected published rules.
[Pocket](https://pocket.funclub.com.tw/rule) supplies the common tai and flower
awards; [EASTKING](https://www.eastking.com.tw/blog/posts/mahjong-south-north-differences)
supplies Southern differences. [Funtown](https://www.funtown.com.tw/mj/intro/intro5.html)
supplies opening, calls, reserve and payment conventions;
[Mahjong Star](https://mahjongstar.waningames.com/Games/Game_Rule) supplies the
nearest-winner and passing rules. The table uses Funtown kong eligibility and
does not adopt Mahjong Star's additional chi/pong passing restrictions.

Implementation contracts, source qualifications and regression ownership remain
in [Architecture](ARCHITECTURE.md) and [Taiwanese mahjong design](TAIWAN_DESIGN.md).
